# Referee report — Reviewer 2

**Manuscript:** "BARCS generalizes beta-binomial CRISPR-screen inference to
longitudinal and covariate-adjusted designs"

**Recommendation:** Major revision.

Every numeric claim below was checked against the manuscript source, the
committed result tables under `results/`, the analysis scripts under
`examples/`, and the implementation in `R/bbreg.R`. Where I could not verify a
number because the corresponding table is not deposited, I say so.

---

## Summary

The manuscript proposes BARCS, a guide-level beta-binomial regression for
pooled CRISPR screens that replaces the two group means of CB<sup>2</sup> with
an arbitrary model matrix, and evaluates it on two longitudinal screens, an
ordered-bin FACS screen, two simulators, and several audits.

The premise is sound and the writing is, in places, unusually candid: the
manuscript states that it does not claim to outperform CB<sup>2</sup> on binary
designs, that concordance is not evidence of better fit, that the Liang inputs
are not literal counts, that post-fit CNV correction over-corrects HT-29, and
that the DeWeirdt rerun largely failed. I want to say clearly that this candor
is a strength and should survive revision.

My difficulty is that the candor is confined to the Methods, Limitations and
Supplement, while the Abstract, Discussion and Conclusion are written as
though the benchmarks were competitive wins. Four of the five quantitative
claims in the Abstract are BARCS-versus-BARCS ablations (adding day 7, adding
moderation, changing the denominator, applying control calibration); the fifth
(HT-29) is a parity result; and the one head-to-head calibration comparison
rests on a quantity that is fixed by construction. Below I set out what I think
must change before the central claims are supportable.

---

## Major concerns

### M1. The headline calibration result is circular

`bb_calibrate_controls()` (`R/bbreg.R:523-604`) estimates a single scale by
matching the empirical `1 - alpha` quantile of the absolute control *t*
statistics to the two-sided Student *t* cutoff at the same `alpha`, with
`alpha = 0.05` by default. The IL2RA script
(`examples/waterbear_facs_benchmark.R:102-105`) passes **all** 593
non-targeting guides as `control`, and
`results/waterbear_facs/non_targeting_calibration.csv` then reports the
`p < 0.05` rate **on those same 593 guides**.

After that scaling, the fraction of control guides with `p < 0.05` is 0.05 by
definition, up to quantile interpolation and the `min_scale` truncation. The
deposited value is 0.0489. So the statement in the Abstract, in
`sections/results/0_calibration.tex:16-19`, and in Table 2 — "reducing the
non-targeting *p* < 0.05 rate from 0.133 to 0.051" — is the estimator's own
fixed point evaluated on its training set. It is not a measurement of
calibration and carries no information about it.

This is not a subtle omission, because the manuscript applies the correct
standard elsewhere. The simCRISPR benchmark splits each control class in half
so that "no guide used to fit the null was also scored against it"
(`sections/methods/6_benchmark-simcrispr.tex:38-42`), and MAUDE's competing
figure is explicitly described as coming from "a held-out-control analysis"
(`sections/results/3_facs-ordered-bin.tex:66-68`). BARCS is therefore held to
an in-sample standard and MAUDE to a held-out one, in the same comparison.

**Required.** Split the non-targeting guides, calibrate on one half, report the
held-out half, and report the sampling variability of the scale across splits.
Also report the whole null distribution rather than one tail probability: the
same deposited file gives a genomic inflation factor of 0.826 after
calibration, which says the bulk of the null is now over-corrected while the
5% point is nominal by construction. That over-correction is the most likely
explanation for losing a validated regulator (23/26 raw to 22/26 calibrated),
and it should be discussed rather than left implicit.

Two further points on the procedure itself, which need to be in the Methods.
First, `min_scale = 1` means calibration can only inflate standard errors,
never deflate them; it is a one-sided device, which makes "calibrated" and
"conservative" partly synonymous. Second, a single global scale cannot repair a
*design-induced* dependence such as the negative dependence between FACS bins
from one donor — it rescales every guide by the same factor regardless of
abundance or dispersion. The manuscript describes the calibration as making the
independence violation "visible and correctable"; only the first half of that
is demonstrated.

### M2. A type-I error study exists in this repository, BARCS fails it, and it is not reported

`results/simulation_summary.csv` — generated by `examples/simulation.R` — is
the only place in the project where nominal and actual error rates are compared
directly. It reports, at gene level:

| Method | Null type I at 0.05 | Empirical FDR at nominal 0.05 |
|---|---|---|
| naive binomial *z* | 0.844 | 0.770 |
| beta-binomial *t* | **0.081** | **0.130** |
| official MAGeCK-MLE Wald | 0.038 | 0.049 |

The beta-binomial *t* is anti-conservative at the nominal 5% level and its
realized false-discovery proportion is 2.6× the nominal 0.05, while the
negative-binomial comparator is calibrated on both. None of this appears in the
manuscript.

I recognise two caveats and do not want to overstate the point: that CSV dates
from the first commit while `examples/simulation.R` was modified later, so it
may be stale; and the `coverage_95` column in that file uses a gene standard
error back-derived from the combined *z*, so I would not treat coverage as
clean. Neither caveat helps, because the manuscript contains **no** direct
nominal-versus-actual error-rate study of its own. For a paper whose thesis is
that the beta-binomial is the better-calibrated sampling model, that is the
missing first table.

**Required.** Simulate from the stated generative model — Equations (2)-(3) —
and report type-I error and realized FDP against nominal levels across the
number of independent libraries *m* ∈ {4, 6, 8, 12}, realistic ranges of *N*,
ρ and guide abundance, at guide level and gene level, with and without
dispersion moderation, with and without control calibration. This is the
experiment that would establish the paper's central claim, and it is cheap.

There is a plausible mechanism to test at the same time. `ρ̂` is truncated at
zero (`R/bbreg.R:120-124`): whenever a guide's Pearson statistic falls below
*m* − *q*, the weights revert to purely binomial and the standard error is too
small. With *m* − *q* between 2 and 6 that will happen for a large share of
guides, which would explain both the 0.081 above and the uncalibrated 0.133
non-targeting rate in the IL2RA screen. Please report the fraction of guides at
the ρ̂ = 0 boundary in each benchmark and the null rate conditional on it.

### M3. The flagship longitudinal design violates the manuscript's own independence requirement

Limitation 1 states that "repeated measures, matched samples, or multiple
libraries from one biological unit require a subject-level correlation model or
a blocked permutation." The HT-29 analysis then does exactly what that
prohibits. After summing the three sequencing columns per harvest — correctly,
and for the stated reason — the eight columns are one plasmid pool plus seven
serial harvests of a *single continuously passaged HT-29 culture*. Those are
seven repeated measurements of one biological unit with a shared lineage and
autocorrelated drift, not seven independent libraries, yet they are referred to
*t*<sub>6</sub> as if independent. The argument made against the three
sequencing columns applies with less force, but it still applies, to the seven
days.

The Cas13 analysis is better in this respect — five cell lines, replicate
blocks where available — but the K562 fit drops the replicate block entirely
(`sections/methods/7_benchmark-liang.tex:22-26`), which changes the residual
df and the estimand in exactly the cell line that shows the largest claimed
calibration gain (null rates 22.4-33.9% for the alternatives).

**Required.** For HT-29, either fit a correlated-error structure over days,
or use blocked permutation of the day labels to obtain the reference
distribution, or state plainly that df = 6 is optimistic and report the
permutation-based *p*-values alongside. Separately, pDNA is a plasmid pool, not
a cell-derived time point; placing it at day 0 on a logit-linear time axis
assumes no transfection or early-selection bottleneck. Please show the fit with
pDNA excluded.

### M4. The Abstract's Liang comparison is selective

The Abstract reports BARCS's own improvement (average precision 0.776 → 0.786;
recall 0.487 → 0.580), then reports the one metric on which BARCS beats
MAGeCK-MLE (calibration error 0.035 versus 0.103), then describes the ranking
comparison as "retaining comparable essential-gene ranking."

The project README is more forthcoming (lines 198-205): "MAGeCK-MLE has higher
average precision (0.833) and FDR-thresholded recall (0.690)." Against BARCS's
0.786 and 0.580, that is a 6% relative deficit in average precision and a 19%
relative deficit in thresholded recall. Calling this "comparable" while quoting
BARCS's 0.580 and omitting MAGeCK's 0.690 from the same sentence is not a
defensible framing. The Abstract should state both directions, as the README
does.

### M5. The Liang benchmark cannot test the claim the paper makes, and the paper elsewhere says so

The thesis is that the beta-binomial is better motivated because *N*<sub>i</sub>
is a known sequencing total, so that Equation (5) separates depth-dependent
sampling variance from between-library heterogeneity. The deposited Liang values
are fractional after median-of-ratios normalization, ComBat correction and
replicate-outlier processing, and were rounded to integers before fitting. After
those transformations *N*<sub>i</sub> is not a sequencing depth and the
(1 − ρ)/*N* term has no interpretation; what is fitted is a beta-binomial-shaped
quasi-likelihood on transformed data.

The manuscript acknowledges this three times, and then makes the analysis "the
principal evidence for generalization" (Discussion, `sections/6_discussion.tex`).
The difficulty is that the FDR audit uses precisely this argument to *discount*
the Chronos preprint's evidence against CB<sup>2</sup>: "that transformation is
meaningful for Chronos, but it changes the effective sequencing depths of a
method whose likelihood treats read totals as sampling information. Equal
preprocessing is therefore not automatically equivalent to equal model input"
(`sections/results/8_fdr-audit.tex:60-66`). That reasoning cannot be applied to
a competitor's negative result and withheld from the paper's own headline.

There is a second, separable problem. ComBat was applied upstream over some
batch variable that is not specified here. If that variable is correlated with
day, correction can absorb or distort the tested time slope; and the analysis
then adds a replicate block on top, risking double adjustment. State what
ComBat adjusted for and show the slope with and without the replicate block.

**Required.** The repository already contains the raw-read pipeline for
PRJNA1344834 (`scripts/count_liang_cas13_run.sh`,
`scripts/prepare_liang_cas13.R`). Run it and report the raw-count result. If
that is not feasible for this submission, the Liang analysis must be demoted
from "principal evidence" to a sensitivity analysis in both the Abstract and
the Discussion.

### M6. Comparator sets differ across benchmarks, and the omitted comparators are the ones that win

The genome-scale CRISPulator benchmark compares only MAGeCK-MLE and
CRISPhieRmix, justified as "CRISPR-specific gene callers rather than general
count regressions, because that is the choice a screen analyst faces"
(`sections/methods/5_benchmark-crispulator.tex:96-98`). But edgeR-QL, DESeq2
and limma-voom *are* run in the Liang benchmark, and DESeq2 appears in the
CRISPulator pipeline anyway as CRISPhieRmix preprocessing. So the stated
justification is not applied consistently.

It also happens that this repository contains the excluded analysis.
`docs/barcs-external-method-comparison.md` runs those three models on
CRISPulator and reports edgeR-QL with the highest AUROC (0.941) and directional
recall 0.835, and concludes: "On F1 the three count models are not
distinguishable from BARCS-original once the boundary setting is removed." The
README adds a matched-FDP comparison in which edgeR-QL at nominal FDR 0.01
attains F1 0.796 at realized FDP 0.087, against BARCS-original at nominal 0.10
with F1 0.702 at FDP 0.085 — and calls this "a real guide-level
information-borrowing advantage."

The manuscript instead states that "BARCS-MOD led at every shared operating
point" (`sections/results/4_crispulator.tex:38-45`), over a comparator set that
excludes the models the repository documents as winning. I accept that those
figures are for BARCS-original at 400 genes rather than BARCS-MOD at 10,000, so
the comparison is not settled — which is exactly why it belongs in the paper.

**Required.** Run edgeR-QL, DESeq2 and limma-voom in the genome-scale
CRISPulator benchmark and include them in Figure 4 and the matched-FDP
interpolation, or state the exclusion in the Results together with what
`docs/barcs-external-method-comparison.md` found.

### M7. The interaction benchmark has no valid comparator

Table 3's only external method returns F1 = 0.006 and 1.7 calls from roughly
500 substantial interactions, and the manuscript itself explains at length why
the design is outside MAGeCK-MLE's scope. That is a fair explanation, and it
also means Table 3 is entirely a BARCS-versus-BARCS ablation. The Abstract's
"the appropriate control-guide denominator increased F1 from 0.481 to 0.917" is
therefore a normalization ablation, not a comparison against anything.

Worse for the paper's argument, the finding is not specific to the
beta-binomial. Section text attributes it to library-composition shift under
widespread depletion — "38% of the targeting guides are at least halved in the
treated knockout arm, which inflates the share of every surviving guide." That
mechanism, and the control-normalization remedy, apply identically to any count
model. As written, the result restates why control-based normalization exists.

**Required.** Add at least one real guide-level baseline fitted to the same
interaction design: edgeR-QL, limma-voom or DESeq2 with the interaction term,
a quasi-binomial GLM, and ideally a beta-binomial fitted by full maximum
likelihood (see M8). Only then can the contribution of the beta-binomial
sampling model be separated from the contribution of the denominator choice.

### M8. Existing beta-binomial regression software is not cited or compared

The manuscript is explicit and correct that the construction is Williams (1982)
extra-binomial variation plus the Baggerly (2004) regression bridge. The
consequence is that the model itself is established, and several
implementations of it exist: `aod::betabin`, `VGAM::betabinomial`,
`gamlss.dist::BB`, and — closest to this work — **corncob** (Martin, Witten &
Willis, *Ann. Appl. Stat.* 2020), which is beta-binomial regression of counts
on library totals with a logit-linked mean and a modeled dispersion, developed
for microbiome sequencing. I checked `main.bib`: none of these is cited.

This matters beyond attribution. The manuscript's own stated weakness is that
Equation (12) is a plug-in covariance that "does not propagate uncertainty from
estimating a separate dispersion for every guide"
(`sections/methods/3_inference.tex:8-20`). A likelihood fit with observed
information, or a REML/bias-corrected dispersion, addresses exactly that. The
paper chooses the weaker option, presumably for speed at 50,000-90,000 guides,
which is a legitimate trade — but the cost has to be measured, not asserted.

**Required.** Cite the existing implementations; soften the Introduction's
claim that "a general-purpose, depth-aware coefficient test for pooled CRISPR
designs remains missing" to reflect that such regression software exists and
what is missing is the screen-specific contract; and benchmark against at least
one full-ML beta-binomial fit on accuracy, calibration and runtime. Scope the
novelty claim to the contract you actually contribute — immutable unfiltered
totals, control calibration, dispersion moderation, gene aggregation, and speed.

### M9. BARCS-MOD, the statistic carrying the Abstract's simulation claims, is never defined

The Abstract and `sections/results/4_crispulator.tex:29-36` attribute F1
0.811 → 0.847 and average precision 0.902 → 0.919 to a procedure described only
as moderating "each guide's dispersion toward an abundance-dependent trend,"
with "42.7-45.8 prior degrees of freedom." No equation, prior specification,
trend estimator, or derivation of the prior df appears anywhere in the Methods.
Section 3 defines the *gene-level* empirical-Bayes shrinkage (Equations 26-27),
which is a different object used for different statistics.

The implementation (`R/bbreg.R:690-800`) is a limma-style scaled-F moderation of
the Pearson variance inflation with a trigamma moment estimator, replacing the
residual df by `df_residual + prior_df`, and it has a `one_way` option whose
`pmax(moderated, fitted_inflation)` makes the moderated test never more liberal
than the unmoderated one. All of that must be in the Methods, including:

1. the full estimator, prior, and trend specification;
2. whether the one-sided truncation was active in the reported runs — a
   moderation that can only increase the standard error is a deliberately
   conservative device, and it changes how the realized-FDP results should be
   read;
3. why moderation is applied in the two simulations but **not** in the HT-29,
   IL2RA or Liang analyses, which use control scaling instead.

Point 3 is the substantive one. The paper has two distinct remedies for the same
miscalibration, applies each to a disjoint set of benchmarks, and never compares
them. Please report both remedies on the same data.

### M10. F1 at a nominal threshold is the deciding metric, and it rewards liberal calling

No primary endpoint is prespecified and each benchmark reports a different
metric set (AUROC/PR AUC/NNMD/Recall<sub>90</sub>/FP<sub>10%</sub>; AP/recall/F1/FDP;
recall/F1/null/safe-harbour; AUROC/AP/F1/CNV; Spearman/Jaccard). F1 recurs
wherever BARCS leads.

Sanson A375 shows the problem cleanly. From
`results/sanson_benchmark/benchmark_metrics.csv` and
`paired_bootstrap_comparison.csv`: AUROC difference 0.0032 (95% CI −0.0027 to
0.0092) and average precision difference −0.0000056 (95% CI −0.0035 to 0.0030)
— both indistinguishable from zero. The entire reported advantage is +4.18
percentage points of recall and +0.0253 F1 at nominal FDR 0.05. That is a
statement that BARCS calls more genes, on an evaluation set that is 1,506
essential versus 869 nonessential, i.e. 63% positive prevalence, where F1
mechanically rewards liberal calling.

Whether that extra recall is power or anti-conservatism is decidable and is not
reported: the realized false-positive rate among reference nonessential genes at
nominal FDR 0.05 is absent for both methods. Given M1 and M2, this cannot be
left open.

**Required.** Report realized FDP at the nominal threshold for every method in
every table; prespecify one primary metric per benchmark; and for Sanson, state
that ranking performance is indistinguishable from MAGeCK-MLE.

### M11. Most comparisons carry no uncertainty

Only Sanson has confidence intervals, and the machinery to produce them (paired
stratified bootstrap) is already written. Elsewhere:

- Table 1 reports AUROC to four decimals with differences of 0.0003 and asserts
  they are "within the margins discussed in the text" without ever giving a
  margin.
- CRISPulator reports means over three seeds; average precision 0.902 versus
  0.919 and F1 0.811 versus 0.847 are then treated as ordered.
- simCRISPR reports means over three seeds, with F1 0.870 versus 0.917 read as
  an improvement.

**Required.** Per-seed values and seed-level intervals for both simulators;
bootstrap intervals for Table 1. With three seeds, say so and avoid ranking
language that three seeds cannot support.

### M12. Table 1 excludes the best-performing method on that benchmark

`results/chronos_tzelepis/benchmark_metrics.csv` contains five rows. The
deposited single-endpoint "Published MAGeCK day 25" has the highest AUROC
(0.97925) and the highest recall at 90% precision (0.9120) of all five, and
"Published BAGEL2 day 25" has the fewest false positives (4). Table 1 keeps
only the three full-time-course fits, and the endpoint results are moved into a
paragraph about how "a multi-endpoint result is reduced to one number."

That paragraph is a fair point about reduction contracts, but it also removes
the most important comparison in the paper from the reader's view. On this
benchmark, the manuscript's own numbers indicate that fitting all eight columns
buys nothing over analysing day 25 alone — which is the natural null hypothesis
for the entire longitudinal argument.

**Required.** Put all five rows in Table 1 and address the comparison
head-on. If the position is that the deposited day-25 analyses are not
contract-matched, then the honest statement is that HT-29 cannot separate
longitudinal from endpoint analysis, and the burden shifts entirely onto the
Liang screen — which brings M5 back into play.

### M13. Lemma 1 is a tautology; the informative version is empirical and is not reported

In Lemma 1, Ω is constructed *from* CB<sup>2</sup>'s own weights
*a*<sub>i</sub>, *b*<sub>i</sub> and variances *V*<sub>A</sub>, *V*<sub>B</sub>.
A saturated two-cell GLS whose weights are chosen to reproduce two given group
summaries necessarily reproduces them. The manuscript concedes the point
("should not be interpreted as proof that the implemented `bbreg()` estimator
strictly nests the original estimator"), which leaves unclear what the page of
algebra and the machine-precision check on a single deterministic guide
establish.

The informative statement is empirical: the distribution of the discrepancy
between `bbreg(~ group)` and `measure_sgrna_stats()` across every guide of a
real two-group screen. Sanson A375 is already in the repository and bundled with
CB<sup>2</sup>.

Relatedly — and this surprised me in a manuscript framed as generalizing
CB<sup>2</sup> — CB<sup>2</sup> appears in no benchmark table except the FDR
audit. It is absent from Table 4 (Sanson), which is a two-group endpoint screen
where CB<sup>2</sup> is the natural comparator and where the equivalence claim
is directly testable.

**Required.** Replace or supplement Lemma 1 with the empirical discrepancy
distribution, and add CB<sup>2</sup> to the Sanson benchmark.

### M14. Proposition 1's assumptions exclude the case that matters most

The proposition requires that "their groupwise and pooled precision estimators
converge to a common κ." BARCS estimates one ρ across the whole design;
CB<sup>2</sup> estimates dispersion separately within each group. When ρ differs
between conditions the two statistics differ at first order, and BARCS's
single-ρ model is misspecified rather than merely different.

Condition-dependent dispersion is not a corner case. The manuscript invokes it
itself when explaining the DeWeirdt failure: "with two replicates per arm, a
treatment-associated quality shift is nearly collinear with treatment itself"
(`sections/results/8_fdr-audit.tex:108-112`). So the paper's own explanation for
its weakest result is the condition the equivalence proposition assumes away.

**Required.** State the restriction explicitly where the proposition is given;
simulate ρ<sub>A</sub> ≠ ρ<sub>B</sub> and report the effect on type-I error and
power; and say whether a dispersion-by-group or dispersion-link extension is
feasible in this framework.

## Specific and minor points

**Abstract and Table 2 (IL2RA).**

1. Calibration *reduced* validated recovery from 23/26 to 22/26. The Abstract
   quotes the post-calibration 22/26 as a positive result without noting either
   the cost or that Waterbear (24/26) and MAUDE (25/26) both recover more.
2. Table 2 compares recovery at each method's own threshold — 49, 72, 79 and
   406 discoveries. Recovery at unmatched call counts is not a comparison. Add
   recovery at a matched discovery count (for example the top 49 for every
   method) or a precision-recall curve over the 26 validated genes.
3. The "NTC *p* < 0.05" column is populated only for the three BARCS rows and
   is "---" for every competitor, yet it is the column the argument depends on.
   The calibration subsection already gives MAUDE's held-out 0.095 — put it in
   the table, and compute the equivalent for MAGeCK-MLE (declaring the NTCs as
   genes) and for Waterbear. As it stands, BARCS's uncalibrated 0.133 is worse
   than MAUDE's held-out 0.095, and its calibrated 0.051 is in-sample; the
   table shows neither fact.

**Numbers that do not match the deposited results.**

4. Table 2 reports 30 discoveries for "MAGeCK test, outer bins";
   `results/waterbear_facs/benchmark_metrics.csv` reports 32.
5. The calibrated non-targeting rate is given as 0.051 in the Abstract, Table 2
   and `sections/results/0_calibration.tex`; the deposited value is 0.0489.
6. Both discrepancies are consistent with the deposited CSV predating the text
   (last touched 2026-07-24 against 2026-07-29 for the section). Regenerate and
   reconcile before resubmission.

**Deposited evidence covers the secondary analyses only.**

7. `results/` is gitignored with per-file exceptions. The deposited tables cover
   HT-29, Sanson, GSE70038 and IL2RA — the analyses the manuscript itself labels
   as robustness checks — while the Liang, CRISPulator and simCRISPR summary
   tables, which carry every quantitative claim in the Abstract, are absent. A
   reviewer cannot check the primary results. Please deposit them.

**Metric definitions.**

8. Liang: a macro-averaged null rate of 0.076 gives 0.026, not the reported
   0.035, so "mean absolute calibration error" must be a per-cell-line mean of
   |rate − 0.05|. For MAGeCK the two coincide exactly (0.153 − 0.05 = 0.103),
   which makes the two numbers look computed differently. Define the metric and
   give the per-cell-line table.
9. NNMD's sign convention and the 10%-tail false-positive definition are
   introduced in the Results prose; move them to the Methods.
10. simCRISPR positives are guides with |INT| ≥ 0.2, "fixed before scoring."
    Recall and F1 depend directly on that cut. Show Table 3 at 0.1 and 0.3.

**Citations.**

11. MAUDE (`deboer2020maude`), BAGEL2 (`kim2021bagel2`) and RRA
    (`kolde2012rra`) are used as comparators or discussed in the text but never
    cited; those bib entries are unused, as are `marioni2008rnaseq` and
    `spahn2017pinaplpy`. MAUDE and BAGEL2 in particular appear in tables.

**Structure.**

12. The cross-benchmark calibration subsection
    (`sections/results/0_calibration.tex`) is the paper's central argument and
    supplies numbers quoted in the Abstract, yet it sits in the Supplement.
    Move it into the main Results.

**Text and reporting.**

13. `sections/methods/4_benchmark-ht29.tex:26` contains a broken sentence: "...
    file identifiers are 26548532, 26548541, 30847513, 26548550, and 26548553.
    As / Those two matrices carry one column per day". A clause is missing.
14. Software section: give the hardware, R version, and whether the compiled or
    base-R path produced the quoted 4.73 s and 21.4 s timings. Also, 86,840 of
    86,882 fits "reached the strict convergence tolerance" — say what happened
    to the other 42 and whether they enter the benchmark.
15. Limitation 6 cites "the 50 supporting 400-gene CRISPulator runs," a
    configuration that does not appear in the Methods, which describe three
    seeds at 10,000 genes plus a ten-scenario, five-seed sensitivity analysis.
    Reconcile.
16. Table 3's default configuration — the library denominator, which is what
    the software does out of the box — has null rates of 0.004 and 0.000 at
    nominal FDR 0.10 and roughly half the recall of the control denominators.
    The default is therefore severely conservative. Say so in the Software

17. GSE70038: a top-200 Jaccard of 0.53-0.56 means about 45% of each method's
    top hits are not shared, which the text does not engage with while reading
    the Spearman values as validation. Concordance in the strongly depleted tail
    is also inflated by the common essential genes. Report concordance
    stratified by effect magnitude and discuss the top-200 disagreement.

## What I would need to see to support publication

1. Held-out control calibration wherever calibration is reported (M1).
2. A direct type-I error and realized-FDP study under the paper's own
   generative model, across *m*, *N*, ρ and abundance (M2).
3. Either the raw-count Liang analysis, or the Liang screen demoted from
   principal evidence in the Abstract and Discussion (M5).
4. Consistent comparator sets: general count regressions in the genome-scale
   simulation, a real baseline in the interaction simulation, at least one
   existing beta-binomial regression implementation cited and benchmarked
   (M6, M7, M8).
5. A complete Methods specification of BARCS-MOD, and one benchmark where
   moderation and control calibration are compared on the same data (M9).
6. Realized FDP and uncertainty intervals in every results table (M10, M11).
7. An Abstract that matches the numbers, including the metrics on which
   MAGeCK-MLE, Waterbear and MAUDE do better (M4, points 1-3).

A closing remark, offered constructively. Read as a generalization paper —
"here is beta-binomial regression for screen designs CB<sup>2</sup> cannot
express, it achieves parity on established designs, and here are ablations
showing which components of the analysis contract matter" — this is a useful
and largely well-executed contribution, and most of the honest version of that
story is already written in the Limitations and the Supplement. Read as the
