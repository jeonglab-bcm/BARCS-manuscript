BARCS generalizes the depth-aware beta-binomial principle of
CB\textsuperscript{2} from a two-group comparison to longitudinal,
covariate-adjusted, and factorial pooled-screen designs.  It conditions each
guide count on the unfiltered library total, assigns inferential degrees of
freedom to independent libraries, and reports a prespecified coefficient or
contrast.

The evidence supports a focused conclusion.  BARCS achieved parity with
specialist longitudinal methods in HT-29, used all three time points in the
Liang Cas13 screens, improved validated-regulator recovery in a
donor-adjusted ordered-bin analysis, and led F1 at matched realized error rates
in genome-scale simulation.  These results establish the regression
generalization and its calibrated performance across multiple screen designs.

The remaining priorities are a hierarchical gene model, correlation-aware
inference for repeated biological units and compositional bins, nonlinear time
and dose effects, and raw-count confirmation of the Liang analysis.  Within
its stated domain, BARCS supplies the missing link between
CB\textsuperscript{2}'s beta-binomial sampling model and modern multivariable
screen designs.
