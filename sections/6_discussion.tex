BARCS extends the beta-binomial sampling principle established by
CB\textsuperscript{2}.  Pooled-guide counts are represented as proportions
conditional on observed library totals, and the mean is generalized from two
groups to an arbitrary design matrix.  The resulting estimand is an ordinary
coefficient: a time slope, dose response, adjusted treatment effect,
interaction, or prespecified contrast.

Goodness-of-fit comparisons in the original CB\textsuperscript{2} study
favored the beta-binomial representation of pooled-guide counts
\citep{jeong2019beta}.  The present results extend that advantage to
regression designs.  BARCS maintained comparable essential-gene
discrimination in HT-29 and substantially improved longitudinal null
calibration in the Liang screens while using the known library denominator
directly.

The two longitudinal analyses are the principal evidence for generalization.
HT-29 demonstrates that BARCS can analyze eight time points without reducing
them to an endpoint contrast.  The Liang reanalysis uses days 0, 7, and 14
jointly across five cell lines, rather than the previous day-0/day-14
comparison.  Together they show that the method performs the analysis that
CB\textsuperscript{2} cannot express.  The IL2RA experiment extends the same
argument to an ordered phenotype with donor adjustment, where intermediate
bins improved validated-regulator recovery over an outer-bin comparison.

The simulations refine the performance claim.  BARCS-MOD provided the best F1
at matched realized false-discovery proportions across the evaluated
operating points.  The factorial simulation further showed that replacing the
full library with prespecified control guides increased interaction F1 from
0.481 to 0.917.  The sampling model, normalization contract, dispersion
estimator, and calibration procedure therefore form one analysis pipeline.

The external false-discovery audit reinforces the importance of that contract
(Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:chronosaudit}).  Preserving immutable
full-library totals reduced the Avana null-only result from 152 reported false
discoveries to zero on the identical evaluation genes.  In the independent
PSN1 null split, BARCS produced 4.81\% guide $p<0.05$ and no gene discoveries.
These results show that the denominator must remain part of the
beta-binomial sampling model after guide filtering.

The appropriate method remains design dependent.  CB\textsuperscript{2} is a
direct choice for one prespecified two-group contrast.  BARCS is appropriate
when independent libraries lie on a quantitative or multivariable design.
Waterbear is preferable when joint FACS-bin dependence is central, and
Chronos is preferable when knockout timing and population dynamics are the
scientific model.  Negative controls should be used for calibration when
available, and post-fit copy-number correction should be accepted only after a
null-gene diagnostic.  These are not exceptions to the main claim; they define
its domain.
