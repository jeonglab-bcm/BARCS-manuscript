\subsection{Cross-benchmark calibration diagnostics}
\label{sec:calibration}

Prespecified negative controls provide an empirical check on reported
$p$-values.  Under a calibrated null, approximately 5\% of non-targeting
guides, safe-harbor targets, simulated null genes, or non-expressed
transcripts should satisfy $p<0.05$.  This diagnostic complements ranking and
recall because a method can gain apparent sensitivity by returning
anti-conservative probabilities.

The clearest experimental example was the IL2RA FACS screen.  The uncalibrated
BARCS fit yielded $p<0.05$ for 0.133 of non-targeting guides; negative-control
calibration reduced that fraction to 0.051 while retaining 22 of 26 validated
regulators.  Calibration is therefore not an automatic consequence of the
estimator: it depends on the complete analysis contract, including the
denominator, design, and use of controls.

The longitudinal Liang analysis led to the same distinction.  Across five cell
lines, BARCS had a macro-averaged null $p<0.05$ rate of 0.076 and hence an
absolute calibration error of 0.035.  The matched longitudinal MAGeCK-MLE
analysis had a null rate of 0.153 and an error of 0.103.  General RNA-seq count
models achieved higher ranking sensitivity but had null rates between 0.139
and 0.153.

These results argue for reporting empirical null behavior alongside power,
not for selecting one method in every design.  BARCS was comparatively
well calibrated among procedures estimating the same longitudinal slope.
