\subsection{The organising result: whether a stated error rate is real}
\label{sec:calibration}

Every screen analysed below that supplies prespecified negative controls
supplies the same test, and it is the test this manuscript is organised around.
A method reports a nominal $p$-value; the controls state how often it should be
exceeded.  Guides or genes that are inactive by construction---non-targeting
sequences, safe-harbor cuts, simulated null genes, non-expressed
transcripts---should cross $p<0.05$ in five percent of cases.  The fraction that
actually does is a direct, assumption-free measurement of whether the advertised
error rate is the delivered one.

Each benchmark below reports that measurement for its own controls, and two
patterns run through them.  The first is that control-calibrated BARCS does not
exceed its nominal rate on any real screen analysed here: 0.049 among 593
non-targeting guides in the IL2RA FACS screen, 0.047 on the PSN1 null split,
0.053 among simulated control genes at genome scale, and 0.065 macro-averaged
over the five Liang cell lines.  The second is that this is not a property of
the estimator.  The same estimator on the same IL2RA screen is inflated at 0.133
without control calibration, and CB\textsuperscript{2} on the Avana null-only
benchmark is inflated at 0.145 when the library denominator is recomputed after
guide filtering.  Calibration is a property of the analysis contract, of which
the estimator is one term.

Section~\ref{sec:liang} makes the comparison against other methods on a single
screen and a full range of thresholds, which is the form in which the claim is
testable rather than merely asserted.

The three general-purpose RNA-seq count models are the informative comparison,
because they are given the identical matrix and the identical design.  On the
five-line macro average their null $p<0.05$ frequencies are 0.103 for DESeq2,
0.118 for limma--voom, and 0.122 for edgeR-QL, against 0.065 for BARCS and
0.121 for MAGeCK-MLE.  Their threshold recall is correspondingly higher, 0.78
to 0.79 against 0.487.  The extra discoveries are therefore bought at roughly
twice the advertised error rate, which is the trade this manuscript is written
to make visible rather than to win.

Two facts are worth stating plainly rather than burying.  Rank aggregation is
better calibrated than BARCS on the Liang benchmark, not worse: 0.035 for the
published \texttt{RobustRankAggreg} analysis and 0.046 for MAGeCK-RRA, both
below nominal, while recovering more essential controls.  And the Avana
false-discovery benchmark remains inflated at 0.145 and 0.117 in two cell lines
even after the library totals are repaired, so preserving the denominator
removes an artifact without establishing calibration on its own.  A calibration
argument that omitted either fact would be weaker for it.
