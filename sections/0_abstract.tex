\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Background.}  CB\textsuperscript{2} established a
beta-binomial model for two-group CRISPR screens, but modern screens often
require time, dose, donor, ordered-bin, or interaction effects.  These designs
cannot be reduced to a binary contrast without discarding information.

\noindent\textbf{Methods.}  BARCS generalizes the CB\textsuperscript{2}
sampling model to guide-level beta-binomial regression,
$\logit(\mu_{gi})=\mathbf{x}_i^\top\boldsymbol{\beta}_g$.  It conditions each
guide count on the unfiltered library total, estimates prespecified
coefficients or contrasts, and optionally uses negative-control calibration
and dispersion moderation.

\noindent\textbf{Results.}  BARCS matched established methods in an HT-29
time-course screen, showing that the generalization preserves performance in
the original screening regime.  In a Cas13 reanalysis using days 0, 7, and 14,
the intermediate time point increased BARCS average precision from 0.776 to
0.786 and FDR-thresholded recall from 0.487 to 0.580; BARCS also had lower null
calibration error than longitudinal MAGeCK-MLE (0.035 versus 0.103), although
retaining comparable essential-gene ranking.  In an IL2RA FACS screen,
calibrated BARCS recovered 22 of 26 validated regulators
while reducing the non-targeting $p<0.05$ rate from 0.133 to 0.051.  In a
10,000-gene simulation, moderation increased F1 from 0.811 to 0.847, and in an
interaction simulation the appropriate control-guide denominator increased F1
from 0.481 to 0.917.

\noindent\textbf{Conclusions.}  BARCS extends beta-binomial CRISPR-screen
inference beyond CB\textsuperscript{2} to longitudinal and
covariate-adjusted designs.  The results support the beta-binomial as a
well-calibrated sampling model for guide counts conditional on library depth
and establish its use for multivariable CRISPR-screen inference.
\end{abstract}
