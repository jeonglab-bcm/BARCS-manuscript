\begin{abstract}
Pooled CRISPR screens increasingly use longitudinal, donor-adjusted, and
factorial designs, whereas beta-binomial screen methods have largely remained
limited to two-group comparisons.  BARCS extends the
library-total-conditional beta-binomial model to guide-level regression with
an arbitrary design matrix, enabling direct estimation of time, covariate, and
interaction effects.  We evaluated BARCS in longitudinal and
ordered-phenotype experiments, multivariable simulations with known truth, and
external false-discovery audits.  In a three-time-point Cas13 analysis, BARCS
distinguished proxy-null guide trajectories that competing count methods
called significant, although alternative methods ranked essential genes more
strongly on the processed matrix.  In an IL2RA FACS screen, donor-adjusted
analysis of all four ordered bins recovered more validated regulators per
discovery than matched MAGeCK-MLE, and held-out non-targeting controls
corrected excess guide-level significance.  Simulations showed gains from
dispersion moderation and control-based denominators under composition
shifts.  Because the recent manuscript by \citet{dempster2025fdr} raised
concerns about excess false discoveries from beta-binomial CRISPR analyses, we
revisited its deposited benchmark.  The audit qualified that concern:
pre-fit guide subsetting altered the effective denominator, and restoring
full-library totals eliminated the reported CB\textsuperscript{2} null
discoveries; independently, BARCS retained a near-nominal guide-level null in
PSN1.  Together, these results support the beta-binomial framework in both
CB\textsuperscript{2} and BARCS when full-library denominators are preserved
and controls are handled appropriately.  BARCS extends this framework from
pairwise to multivariable CRISPR designs; confirmatory use requires
independent biological replication and held-out or cross-fitted controls.
\end{abstract}
