\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Background.}  Pooled CRISPR screens increasingly use
longitudinal, donor-adjusted, and factorial designs, yet beta-binomial screen
methods have largely remained limited to two-group comparisons.

\noindent\textbf{Methods.}  BARCS extends the library-total-conditional
beta-binomial model to guide-level regression with an arbitrary design matrix.
We evaluated longitudinal and ordered-phenotype experiments, multivariable
simulations with known truth, and external false-discovery benchmarks.

\noindent\textbf{Results.}  BARCS estimated a single longitudinal effect across
three Cas13 time points and distinguished several proxy-null guide trajectories
that competing count methods called significant.  In an IL2RA FACS screen,
donor-adjusted analysis of all four ordered bins recovered more validated
regulators per discovery than a matched MAGeCK-MLE fit, while held-out
non-targeting controls exposed and corrected excess guide-level significance.
Simulations showed that dispersion moderation improved BARCS within a common
analysis pipeline and that control-based denominators substantially improved
interaction recovery under global composition shifts.  Results were
dataset-dependent: alternative methods ranked essential genes more strongly in
the processed Cas13 data, and external audits showed that preprocessing and
library-total definitions can change apparent false-discovery behavior.

\noindent\textbf{Conclusions.}  BARCS generalizes the beta-binomial sampling
principle from pairwise screening to multivariable CRISPR designs.  Its main
advantage is direct estimation of time, covariate, and interaction effects
while preserving full-library denominators.  Confirmatory use still requires
independent biological replication, likelihood-compatible counts, and
held-out or cross-fitted controls.
\end{abstract}
