\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Background.}  Pooled CRISPR screens increasingly place
libraries along time, dose, FACS bins, or donors, making the estimand a trend,
adjusted association, or interaction, not a binary contrast.

\noindent\textbf{Methods.}  BARCS fits
$\logit(\mu_{gi})=\mathbf{x}_i^\top\boldsymbol{\beta}_g$ per guide against
unfiltered library totals, with a beta-binomial variance separating
sequencing precision from between-library heterogeneity, and refers a
prespecified coefficient or contrast to $t_{m-q}$ on independent libraries.
Two new additional components could improve accuracy: negative-control
calibration and guide-dispersion moderation.

\noindent\textbf{Results.}  In a primary-T-cell IL2RA FACS screen BARCS
recovered 22 of 26 validated regulators versus 17 for MAGeCK-MLE, calibration
cutting non-targeting $p<0.05$ from 13.3\% to 4.9\%.  In a 10,000-gene
CRISPulator simulation, moderation raised F1 from 0.811 to 0.847, leading
MAGeCK-MLE and CRISPhieRmix at every matched false-discovery proportion.  On
simCRISPR, interaction F1 rose from 0.481 to 0.917 when control guides replaced
the full library as denominator.  Two benchmarks bound the claim: a
longitudinal HT-29 screen gave parity with continuous-time MAGeCK-MLE and
Chronos rather than gain, and the original authors' rank aggregation led a
processed-count Cas13 benchmark with one residual degree of freedom.

\noindent\textbf{Conclusions.}  BARCS bridges CB\textsuperscript{2}'s
two-group test and specialist generative models: transparent, scalable
coefficient inference for quantitative and adjusted designs, with diagnostics
for those it cannot repair.
\end{abstract}
